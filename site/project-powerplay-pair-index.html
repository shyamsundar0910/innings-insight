IPL Powerplay Pair Index.

A framework for comparing opening bowling pairs using wicket threat, economy and dot-ball pressure rather than one metric in isolation.

The idea

Which new-ball pairs create the most complete pressure?

A powerplay bowler can be valuable in different ways. Some attack wickets and concede boundaries. Others suppress scoring but do not break partnerships. The Pair Index combines both dimensions to compare bowling combinations more fairly.

How the ranking works

Each pair is evaluated on shared powerplay output, with wickets rewarding attacking impact, economy rewarding control, and dot-ball percentage capturing pressure that may not immediately appear in the wicket column.

The goal is not to claim that one index is a perfect measure of bowling quality. It is a comparison tool that brings multiple new-ball responsibilities into one view.

Example from the analysis

One of the strongest combinations in the 2026 work was Mohammed Shami with Prince Yadav: 11 wickets, a 7.15 economy rate and a 54.04% dot-ball rate, producing an index score of 84.47 in the ranking framework.

Why pairs instead of individuals?

Powerplay plans are usually constructed as combinations. A strike bowler at one end can operate differently when the other end is applying control. Evaluating the pair helps surface complementary roles that individual leaderboards often miss.
